# ARUMBU Community Domain Reconsideration v1

## Method

The instruction was to try to prove v1 wrong, not to perform having tried. Each question below was worked through by taking the founder's own examples and pushing on them until they either broke the model or confirmed it — the same discipline the Institution Setup Experience Reconsideration used, applied here to five specific, harder questions rather than two open ones. Where v1 survives, the reasoning that survived it is written out in full, not asserted. Where it doesn't, the break is named exactly, and the fix is scoped to the smallest change that actually closes it — never a excuse to redesign more than the flaw requires.

## What remains unquestionably correct

**Contact + Relationship, with Direction universal and Type institution-configured, is the right two-tier shape.** Nothing in any of the five questions below breaks this. Every challenge that landed real ground landed *inside* this shape — as a refinement to what a Contact is, or how a Relationship should read once opened — never as a reason to replace the shape itself.

**The three Directions, as a closed, universal enum, survive Question 1 intact** — see below for why "Partner" doesn't add a fourth.

**The People-boundary rule — access requires a real Person and Membership, nothing else does — survives Question 3 completely.** What changes is only how the boundary is *described*, not where it sits or what triggers crossing it.

**Every named connection point (Finance, Work, Documents, History) survives Question 4** — strengthened, in Finance's case, by a dependency Question 4 makes explicit that v1 had left implicit.

**The Tamizhi boundary survives unchanged** — see the dedicated section below. No new principle was needed; the existing ones already cover the one new surface Question 2 introduces.

## What deserves to be challenged

### Question 1 — Is Partnering a fourth Direction?

Tested against every example given: Company↔Company, University↔Hospital, Temple↔NGO, Government↔Manufacturing Company, Foundation↔School, Church↔Charity, Technology Partner, Media Partner, Research Partner.

The test applied to each: does this relationship move value in a way none of Receiving, Supporting, or Supplying already describes — or does it decompose?

**Most of the list decomposes cleanly into the existing three, concurrently.** A University's relationship with a teaching Hospital, recorded from the University's side: the University provides faculty oversight and curriculum to the Hospital (Receiving, from the University's perspective — the Hospital is on the receiving end of that), while the Hospital simultaneously provides clinical placements the University's program depends on to operate (Supplying, from the University's perspective — the Hospital supplies something the University consumes). That is not one relationship needing a new Direction; it is **one Contact holding two concurrent Relationships with two different existing Directions** — exactly the shape v1 already declared explicitly legal ("A single Contact can hold more than one Relationship concurrently"). A Technology Partner or Media Partner decomposes the same way: value moving both directions between the same two parties is not evidence of a fourth Direction, it is the ordinary, already-modeled case of two Directions co-occurring on one Contact.

**A second cluster of the list isn't a different flow of value at all — it's a status label.** A Foundation funding a School, a Government contracting a Manufacturing Company, a Church working with a Charity: strip away the word "Partner" and each of these is a plain Supporting or Supplying relationship, just an unusually large, formal, or strategically important one. Calling a major vendor a "Preferred Partner" doesn't change that the underlying flow of value is 100% Supplying; it signals that the institution wants to *treat* this relationship with more formality or visibility than an ordinary vendor. That is exactly what Type already exists to carry — an institution can name a Type "Strategic Partner" and attach it to whichever Direction actually fits, the identical mechanism that already lets a temple call a Receiving relationship "Devotee" instead of "Customer." Adding a fourth Direction to capture "this relationship matters more than most" would conflate two different questions — *what is being exchanged* (Direction's job) and *how important is this relationship* (a status property, not a flow) — and blur the one clean test the three Directions currently provide.

**One genuine edge case remains, and it's worth taking seriously rather than folding into the above by force.** A true joint venture — two companies co-developing a product neither could have built alone, sharing both the work and the eventual outcome — resists the "who gave what to whom" framing entirely. Neither party is really *receiving*, *supporting*, or *supplying* the other; the value doesn't transfer between them, it's created jointly and belongs to both. This is real, and it does not fit any of the three Directions or a combination of them. But the honest conclusion isn't "therefore a fourth Direction" — it's that **when a relationship stops being a transfer and becomes a shared undertaking, the thing that needs modeling is no longer the relationship itself, it's the undertaking.** That's a Project, not a Community concept — and v1 already named "a Project scoped to a Relationship" as a future seam into Projects, without realizing at the time that this is precisely the case that seam exists to catch. Community still records the other party as a Contact (there has to be someone to relate to), but the actual partnership — the joint work — lives in Projects, hanging off that Contact, the moment Projects is real.

**Verdict on Question 1: reject the fourth Direction.** It survives not by asserting symmetry but by every example actually testing out as either (a) two existing Directions already legally concurrent on one Contact, (b) a Type-level status label riding on top of an existing Direction, or (c) evidence that the real unit under discussion is a Project, not a Community relationship at all. This is worth stating in the domain review directly, since "Partner" is common enough in real institutional language that leaving it unaddressed would look like an oversight rather than a considered rejection.

### Question 2 — Should Community support Individual and Organization as first-class Contact kinds?

This one breaks v1 as written. The founder's own list — Apple, Microsoft, UNICEF, government departments, universities, hospitals, vendors, suppliers, foundations, churches, temples, NGOs — is a list of organizations, and v1's Contact, modeled implicitly as "the external counterpart to Person," has no honest way to represent any of them.

Pressure-testing what actually breaks if Contact only models individuals: a hospital's relationship with a pharmaceutical supplier is, in the real world, a relationship with **Pfizer**, not with whichever sales representative currently holds that account. If Contact can only be a person, the institution is forced to record "Rohan Mehta, Pfizer" as the Contact — and when Rohan changes jobs, the relationship's history, spend, and standing have nowhere correct to live; they either vanish with him or need manually re-parenting to whoever replaces him, which is exactly the kind of fragile, person-shaped memory the whole People Domain Review already rejected for institutional authority (that's precisely why Position, not the person who holds it, carries responsibility). The same failure repeats for a temple's relationship with an NGO, a school's relationship with a Foundation, a company's relationship with a Government department — in every one of these, the *institution* is the substance of the relationship, and any individual is a point of contact within it, not the relationship itself.

This maps directly onto a distinction the People Domain already solved once, for a different pair of concepts: **Position carries the authority, not the person who happens to hold it; an Organization Contact should carry the relationship, not the individual who happens to be the current point of contact.** The fix is small and exactly bounded: Contact should have two first-class kinds, **Individual** and **Organization**, with an Organization Contact optionally naming one or more individual points of contact — themselves either a lightweight embedded fact ("Rohan Mehta, Account Manager") or, only when the relationship with that specific person genuinely matters on its own, its own linked Individual-kind Contact. Direction and Relationship Type both apply identically regardless of kind — Apple can be Supplying, UNICEF can be Supporting via a grant, a Government Department can be Receiving a company's services — Direction and Contact-kind are orthogonal, and nothing about Direction needs to change to accommodate this.

One tempting but wrong extension, worth rejecting explicitly rather than leaving unconsidered: if the other organization happens to also be an ARUMBU tenant, should its Organization Contact record simply *link* to that tenant's real Institution row, for genuine cross-tenant visibility? No. That would cross the Tenant Architecture's core isolation guarantee (Foundation §3) for a case that will almost never be true — Apple, Microsoft, and UNICEF are not going to be ARUMBU tenants, and building real cross-tenant linking on the strength of the rare case where both sides happen to use the same product would be exactly the kind of speculative complexity this entire engagement has consistently refused to build ahead of a proven need (the same reasoning that already kept Capability single-institution-scoped in the People Domain Review). An Organization Contact stays a simple, institution-scoped record — a name and whatever facts *this* institution keeps about that organization — regardless of whether the other side happens to run ARUMBU too.

**Verdict on Question 2: genuine gap, real fix, narrowly scoped.** Organization becomes a first-class Contact kind alongside Individual. This does not reopen Direction, Type, the People-boundary rule, or any connection point named in v1 — it only makes Contact itself correctly shaped for what most real institutional relationships actually are.

### Question 3 — Is "People is internal, Community is external" accurate?

Not quite, and the imprecision is worth naming even though the underlying boundary itself is completely intact.

The phrase smuggles in a social connotation — "us" versus "them," belonging versus not — that the actual mechanism doesn't claim and shouldn't claim. Take the volunteer coordinator from the Community Domain Review's own resolved ambiguity: once they need to log in and manage shifts, they cross into People and gain a Membership and an Affiliation. Did they become more "internal" in any human sense the moment that happened? No — they're the same volunteer, doing the same work, with the same relationship to the institution's mission; the only thing that changed is that they now need to *act inside the system itself*. Meanwhile a major donor, deeply trusted, consulted regularly, sometimes treated with more day-to-day closeness than an ordinary contractor who does hold a Position — stays in Community permanently, because however central they are to the institution's success, they never operate it.

The precise axis, already implicit in every boundary statement v1 actually made, is **participation in operating the institution, not belonging to it.** People is the domain of whoever needs to act, decide, or be held accountable inside ARUMBU itself. Community is the domain of everyone the institution relates to who does not — regardless of how close, important, or long-standing that relationship is. "Internal vs. external" is a reasonable first approximation for a reader skimming quickly, and doesn't need to be struck from the document, but the domain review should state the sharper version directly rather than leaving the approximation to imply something about belonging that isn't actually the rule.

**Verdict on Question 3: the boundary itself is correct and needs no change — only its description does.** Refine the stated framing from "internal vs. external" to "participation in operating the institution vs. relationship without participation."

### Question 4 — Does the Contact model compose with Finance, Projects, Documents, Reports, Calendar, Procurement, Inventory, and Tamizhi?

Mostly yes, and Question 2's fix turns out to be load-bearing for more of this list than Question 2 alone would suggest — which is itself useful evidence that Question 2's conclusion is correct, arrived at twice, independently, from two different directions.

**Finance** — v1 already named `Expense.payee`/`Income.payer` as the natural future seam into a Community Relationship. That seam quietly assumed the Contact on the other end would usually be an organization (most vendors and most large donors are), without ever saying so. Question 2 makes that assumption explicit and correct instead of implicit and shaky.

**Projects** — composes cleanly regardless of Contact kind; a Project can scope to an Individual or an Organization Contact identically. Unaffected.

**Documents** — the `DocumentRef` placeholder pattern is agnostic to Contact kind. Unaffected.

**Reports** — will eventually want to aggregate Community data ("total given by this donor across the year," "total spend with this vendor") — aggregation like that is only meaningful if the Contact being summed has one stable identity over time. Individual-only Contacts would scatter an organization's real relationship across however many different people happened to be the point of contact at different times, making the aggregation Reports exists to produce quietly wrong. This is a second, independent confirmation that Organization must be first-class — Reports cannot do its job honestly without it.

**Calendar** — not previously named in any frozen document, and worth treating as a genuinely new future module rather than assumed. A Calendar would want to schedule something *with* a Contact — a donor meeting, a patient appointment, a vendor delivery window. This requires nothing new beyond what Finance and Work already require: an optional reference from an Event record to a Contact id. Confirms the pattern already established rather than adding a new one.

**Procurement** — also newly named, and this is the module that most sharply exposes what would have gone wrong if Question 2 had been left unaddressed. Procurement is not a peripheral user of Organization Contacts; it cannot function without them. An RFP, a purchase order, a vendor evaluation — every one of these is conducted against a company, never against an individual standing in for one. If Community had shipped as person-only, Procurement would have been the module that made the gap undeniable, at a much higher cost to fix than catching it here.

**Inventory** — goods received against a purchase order trace back to the same Supplying-direction Organization Contact Procurement already needs. No new requirement beyond what Procurement establishes.

**Tamizhi** — governed entirely by the already-frozen Institution Intelligence Principles; see the dedicated section below for the one new surface Organization Contacts introduce, which needs no new rule, only the existing rule applied to a case that didn't exist in v1.

**The hidden assumption, named honestly:** v1's Contact model, as written, would have worked for Finance and Documents well enough to not obviously break — but it would have silently failed the moment Procurement or Reports were designed against it, because both of those modules exist to operate on organizations, not people, and a people-only Contact has no honest way to be the subject of a purchase order or a spend report. That is real technical debt, caught here before anything was built on top of it rather than discovered mid-way through Procurement's own design later, which is precisely what this reconsideration exists to prevent.

**Verdict on Question 4: confirms Question 2 from a second angle, and finds nothing else.** No other named future module — Finance, Projects, Documents, Reports, Calendar, Procurement, Inventory, Tamizhi — requires anything else new added to the Contact model beyond Organization-as-first-class.

### Question 5 — The six-month test

What should a founder understand immediately, opening a Community record six months in — not what fields exist, what *understanding* should it produce?

Tested against v1 as written: today's model would show a Contact's Direction, Type, and whatever static facts were recorded at creation. That answers "what kind of relationship is this," which matters, but it is a fact sheet, not an understanding — the same shape of gap the Institution Setup Experience Reconsideration found in its own six-month test, and the same fix applies for the same underlying reason.

The honest answer to what a founder actually needs to understand, immediately, is **the shape of the relationship over time** — is it warm or has it gone quiet, what has it actually meant to the institution so far, not as a number but as a story. And ARUMBU already has exactly the mechanism this needs, sitting unused for this purpose: **History.** Community connecting every meaningful Relationship event to the shared `recordHistory()` mechanism was already named in v1 as a write-side connection — this reconsideration finds that the read side matters at least as much. A Contact's own detail view should be readable as *that Contact's own slice of History* — every gift this donor has given, every expense recorded against this vendor, every time this relationship was touched — narrated in the same plain, actor-first sentences History already uses everywhere else, filtered to just this one relationship.

Worth rejecting explicitly, and for exactly the same reason the Setup Reconsideration rejected progress-as-a-percentage: **no invented "relationship health score."** A number that claims to summarize warmth or importance would feel informative without being earned or true the same way a percentage-complete institution felt informative without meaning anything. The correct answer is a story a person can actually read and trust, built entirely out of a mechanism that already exists and is already disciplined, not a new metric bolted onto Community alone.

**Verdict on Question 5: real refinement, not a new mechanism.** A Contact's detail view should surface as its own filtered History, the moment History exists to filter — this doesn't require Community to invent anything; it requires the eventual UI to read History the way it's always going to need to write to it, which v1 already set up correctly without noticing it had also set up the answer to this question.

## Tamizhi

No new principle is needed anywhere in this reconsideration — every conclusion above either confirms a rule the Institution Intelligence Principles already state, or introduces a new *surface* for a rule that already exists, never a new rule itself.

The one genuinely new surface: with Organization as a first-class Contact kind, there are now two different kinds of ambiguity Tamizhi might notice — two Individual Contacts who might be the same person, and two Organization Contacts who might be the same company under a different name or a renamed division, plus the subtler case of an Individual Contact who might actually be the same person as a named point-of-contact on an Organization Contact. All three are exactly the same situation the existing Principle 6 already governs: Tamizhi may notice and say so, as an ordinary Be Aware suggestion, and must never merge, link, or reclassify a Contact's kind on its own authority. Nothing about Organization Contacts changes what Tamizhi is allowed to do — it only means the existing "never merge automatically" rule now has three cases to apply to instead of one.

Confirmed directly against the founder's own restated list: Tamizhi continues to only observe; never merges contacts automatically, including across the new Individual/Organization distinction; never communicates externally, a rule Question 2 makes if anything more obviously important, since an Organization Contact is exactly the kind of relationship where an unauthorized outbound message would be most damaging; never creates relationships, including never auto-linking an Organization to a tenant Institution record, which Question 2 explicitly rejected as an architecture choice regardless of what Tamizhi might otherwise be capable of inferring; never decides, only recommends — a Contact's own History view, per Question 5, is exactly the kind of surface Tamizhi could eventually annotate ("this relationship has gone quiet") without ever writing to it, read-only, through the same two doors everything else already uses.

## Verdict: refine, not rewrite

Community Domain Review v1's architecture holds. Contact plus Relationship, Direction as a closed universal three-value enum, Type as institution configuration, the People-boundary rule, and every named connection point survive this reconsideration completely intact. Nothing here found a reason to add a fourth Direction, invent a new engine mechanism, or reopen any decision the Cohesion Review or the Institution Intelligence Principles already settled.

**Four specific, bounded refinements to fold into a Community Domain Review v2 before freezing:**

1. **Reject the fourth Direction explicitly, with reasoning, rather than leaving "Partner" unaddressed.** State plainly that Partnering decomposes into concurrent existing Directions, a Type-level status label, or — in the genuine joint-value case — a signal to open a Project against the Contact, never a fourth Direction.
2. **Make Organization a first-class Contact kind alongside Individual**, with an Organization Contact able to name individual points of contact without those individuals being the substance of the relationship. This is the one change with real teeth — confirmed independently by direct examination (Question 2) and by forward-compatibility against Procurement and Reports specifically (Question 4).
3. **Restate the People/Community boundary as participation-in-operating-the-institution versus relationship-without-participation**, rather than internal versus external — the same boundary, described precisely instead of evocatively.
4. **Specify that a Community record's detail view should read as that Contact's own filtered slice of History**, not a static fact sheet and never an invented relationship-health score — the six-month test's actual answer, built entirely from a mechanism v1 already connected for writing and simply hadn't yet been asked to also be read.

None of these four require touching Direction's enum, the People-boundary rule's substance, or any application's existing implementation — there is none yet to touch. All four are additive clarifications and one real, cleanly-bounded structural addition (Organization as a Contact kind), exactly the shape of correction the Institution Setup Experience went through: not "v1 was wrong," but "v1 was right about its shape and stopped one layer before some of its own consequences were fully worked out."
