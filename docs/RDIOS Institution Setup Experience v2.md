# RDIOS Institution Setup Experience v2

## The real problem, stated precisely

Today, a brand-new institution lands on Home and Home says: *"The institution is calm this morning."*

That sentence is **technically correct and psychologically wrong**, and the gap between those two things is the actual problem this document solves — not "onboarding is missing," something more specific.

Home's Attention Engine can only ever say one of two things when Act Now is empty: *everything real is handled*, or *nothing real exists yet*. Those are opposite situations that currently produce the identical sentence. For a five-year-old institution, "calm" is the best possible news. For an institution that is four minutes old, "calm" is a lie of omission — there is real work to do, and Home is staying silent about it because nothing has taught it that *building the institution* is itself a legitimate category of attention.

That's the actual design problem: **the Attention Engine doesn't yet know that a new institution's most urgent decisions are about itself.** Everything below follows from fixing that.

## The core decision: Setup is not a mode. It's Day-1 attention.

Reject, explicitly, the shape most software reaches for by default: a welcome screen, a numbered wizard, a progress bar, a "Skip setup" escape hatch, a distinct visual mode you enter and later exit. All of that is a second product living inside the first one. It fails the simplest test: *it exists because software usually does this, not because it helps anyone understand RDIOS.*

**The commitment:** the same Attention Engine, the same Act Now / Be Aware / History tiers, the same calm card-and-verb language — populated, in an institution's earliest days, with setup-shaped decisions instead of operational ones. There is no separate Setup screen, because Setup is not a place. It is what Home honestly contains when an institution is new. The content changes because the institution changes. Nothing else does.

## What an institution is made of, before any of that

Forget software for a moment. Take any institution — a company, a temple, a mosque, a church, a school, a hospital, an NGO, a foundation, a family office — and ask what survives if every person inside it left. Not the building. Not the org chart, which would need rebuilding from nothing. What's left that makes someone say "the temple still exists, it just needs new trustees," rather than "the temple is gone"?

**It's why the institution exists at all.** A temple's purpose — to house a deity, to hold a community's worship — survives every trustee, every priest, total turnover, indefinitely. A hospital's purpose survives any specific department structure. This is prior to organization, not a variant of it: **organization is the answer to a question — "what does this institution need, in order to do the thing it exists to do?" — and an answer cannot be the beginning, because it presupposes the question.**

That reframes the two decisions this document originally treated as the beginning:

**Invite someone is real and valuable, but not conceptually first.** An institution can meaningfully exist with exactly one person for real time — a solo founder's company is legally and practically real before employee two; a single trustee can meaningfully hold a fledgling trust; a hermit can consecrate a shrine before a second devotee arrives. Coordination requires more than one person. *Existing* doesn't. Invite is the most useful early *practical* decision, not the first *true* one — a real distinction, not a demotion of its value.

**Shape your organization is also downstream, not foundational.** Deciding who reports to whom is itself an answer to a prior, more human question: what does this institution need to do, and what needs to exist to make that possible? The chart is one tool that eventually serves the purpose. It was never the purpose itself.

**So there is a layer underneath both of them: Purpose.** The one sentence answering "why does this exist," true before any person, any position, any department — the only thing every institution on that list shares, independent of how each happens to be organized today.

## What actually deserves to be Act Now on day one — and what doesn't

Applying the same filter as before, now with Purpose correctly placed beneath the other two:

**The true beginning — offered, never forced:**

- **State your purpose, in one sentence.** Not a form, not a required field gating anything else — offered alongside name and type at the moment an institution is created, as naturally as those two already are. If a founder skips it and goes straight to inviting someone, that's correct behavior, not a failure to complete a step; Purpose earns its place by being remembered forever afterward (see Progress, below), not by being mandatory now.

**The two most valuable early practical decisions — both real, both downstream of purpose:**

- **Invite someone.** Already built, already live-verified this engagement. One card, one verb, no form-within-a-form.
- **Shape your organization.** Never "create a Position" on this screen — that word stays confined to Settings, per the already-frozen Language Rule. On Home, this reads as *"Who reports to whom?"* — offering the institution's own starter shape (see Institution Types, below) as a suggestion, editable, skippable, never forced.

**Still fails the filter, for the same reasons as before:**

- **A welcome screen or tour.** Home's existing calm, personalized greeting already is the welcome.
- **Create a first Task, Project, Expense, or Document.** Real, eventually, to the institution that has one — never manufactured on day one to fill a checklist slot. Each belongs to its own application's future first-use design, offered only when a real gap exists, never invented speculatively here.
- **A numbered "Departments" step separate from Organization.** One moment, not two.

## Progress: not a metric — a why, and a story

The founder asked to choose one — percentages, milestones, journeys, chapters, achievements, or nothing — and pushed back, correctly, when the first answer to this question was simply "nothing."

**Rejecting measurement stands, completely.** Every metric option requires a fixed, known-in-advance, countable sequence, and there isn't one — most of what happens after the first few decisions is genuinely institution-specific and open-ended. A percentage over an undefined denominator isn't honest progress, it's decoration pretending to be measurement, and it applies a small constant pressure — *you are incomplete, hurry up* — exactly the tone the Experience Principles forbid on the screen meant to build trust.

**But "where an institution is" doesn't have to mean "how far along it is."** It can mean *what is it, and what has actually happened so far* — an identity question and a narrative question, not a measurement. RDIOS already has half of that answer built and proven: **History**, live-verified this engagement, already narrating "Lakshmi created the institution," "invited Ganesh," "Ganesh joined," in plain sentences. That's genuine orientation — a story, not a score. What it was missing was a destination to be heading toward, which is exactly what makes a story feel like progress rather than a log.

**Purpose is that destination.** One quiet, permanent, optionally-stated sentence, always visible, that turns "here's what's happened" into "here's what's happened, in service of this." Together, Purpose (the why, stated once) and History (the story so far, already built) answer "where is this institution" completely — no number, no step, no badge, nothing new to build beyond one optional sentence at institution creation and one line of ambient Be Aware content.

## Completion and the long lifecycle

There is no "Setup Complete" screen, because there was never a separate mode to exit. It doesn't disappear, transform, or graduate on a schedule. It simply stops generating cards once the gaps it was honestly reporting — no purpose stated (if the founder chose to state one), one member, no shape — stop being true.

**Not an achievement system** — gamification is the wrong register for an institutional operating system. **Not an annual review** — RDIOS's premise is continuous attention, not calendar-driven check-ins.

What carries forward forever, without ever being named a separate feature: the same raw signal that powered the first setup cards — *does this institution's shape match its reality* — never stops being true or false. Purpose itself is the clearest example: stated once, it doesn't expire, doesn't need re-confirming, simply sits in **Be Aware** permanently, the same tier institution name and type already live in. A Position that goes vacant eighteen months from now is the identical fact, in the identical shape, as one that started vacant on day one. If a future "Institution Health" view is ever built, this is why it would feel coherent rather than bolted on — it would be reading the same signals Setup always read, at a different point in the institution's life. Named as a throughline; not committed to as a build.

## Institution types: same mechanism, different content

Should a Temple and a Startup experience identical setup? **The mechanism, yes. The content, no** — and the frozen architecture already has the right place for that difference.

The Product Foundation's Institution Configuration Layer already names **Organization Templates** as a per-institution-type surface. That's where the difference lives:

- A **Temple**'s "shape your organization" suggestion offers a starter shape close to Trustee → Priest → Volunteer Coordinator.
- A **Hospital**'s offers something like Director → Department Head → Attending → Resident, department-scoped.
- A **Startup**'s offers almost nothing — maybe just Founder and Co-Founder — because forcing structure on two people is exactly the premature step this document argues against throughout.
- An **NGO**, a **School**, a **Foundation**, a **Trust** each get their own starter shape, authored once, reused by every institution of that type.

Purpose has no template — it's the one part of setup that is, by definition, unique to each institution, never suggested, never pre-filled. None of this requires a new engine. It requires Organization Templates, already named, already homed, to exist with real content per type.

## Where this lives, architecturally — nothing new

The setup Act Now moments are contributed the way every future application's Act Now items will be: through the Attention Contract already named in the Product Foundation, already seamed into the running prototype (`os/attention/engine.ts` names this exact extension point in its own comments). Identity is already, today, the one live contributor. Purpose is simply one more optional fact captured alongside name and type at institution creation — no new table shape, no new engine, no new tier; it lives in Be Aware exactly the way institution name and type already do.

## The first ten minutes, narrated

**A Temple.** Lakshmi creates "Meenakshi Temple Trust," selects Temple as the type, and — offered, not required — writes one sentence: *"To care for this deity and this community's worship, for as long as either needs us."* She lands on Home. It already says her name and the time of day. One card: *"You're the only one here — nothing runs alone for long. Invite."* She invites Ganesh, the temple's priest. He accepts. History quietly records both moments, and the sentence she wrote sits quietly in Be Aware, unasked-for again, simply there. A second card appears: *"Who reports to whom?"* — the Temple starter shape. She adjusts one line, accepts it. Home goes quiet. It stays quiet until something real happens — and everything it said while it wasn't quiet was true, useful, and hers.

**A Startup.** Two co-founders skip the purpose sentence entirely — they know why they exist, writing it down feels like paperwork today, and that's a completely correct choice, not a missed step. They invite each other. The organization card offers almost nothing to accept, so it's dismissed in one click. Within ninety seconds they're looking at a calm, empty Home that has already told them the truth: nothing else is real yet, and that's fine.

Both founders had a different first ten minutes, one of them skipped a whole layer entirely, and both were correct.

## The six-month test, applied

The moment worth remembering is still History quietly writing *"Ganesh Bhatt joined"* — live-verified, real. What completes it: months later, opening Settings for an unrelated reason and seeing, still there, unprompted, the sentence Lakshmi wrote on day one about why any of this exists at all. Not a reminder. Not a nudge. Just true, and still there, exactly as she left it. That's the moment this whole document exists to protect.

## What this document deliberately does not design

The first-use moment for Work, Money, Projects, and Documents — each belongs to that application's own design pass. A future "Institution Health" view, named as a throughline, not committed to. Any visual treatment of the setup cards or the purpose sentence — Phase 2's territory, not this document's.
