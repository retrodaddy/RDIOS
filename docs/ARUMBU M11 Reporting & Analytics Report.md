## What this milestone was

The founder's own framing: Reports answer "what happened?", Analytics answers "what deserves attention?" — related, but different, and kept separate. Neither owns anything; everything reported already exists somewhere else. No PDF exporter, no dashboards, no charts for decoration, no Business Intelligence platform. Every design decision was tested against the founder's own question — if I were the founder of this institution, would this help me make a better decision today? — and removed where the answer was no.

## Core Model, as built

`applications/reports/types.ts` — a `Report` carries exactly what the brief named: Identity (title, description), Scope/Filters, Generated Date, Generated By, and a frozen Snapshot. Nothing more.

- **Categories are a real closed enum, not free text.** Every other domain built this engagement (Document's `type`, Project's `stage`, Community's Relationship `type`) uses free text with suggestions, because no fixed list honestly captured an institution's real vocabulary. Reports are the one deliberate exception: the brief enumerated exactly nine categories and said "no institution-specific reports, universal only" — so `REPORT_CATEGORIES` is a real TypeScript union, not a datalist.
- **Snapshots are genuinely frozen.** `computeSnapshot()` runs once, at generation time, reading the same providers every Be Aware line already reads (People, Work, Finance, Community, Projects, Documents, History) and writing the result permanently into the Report record. Nothing about a saved Report changes again except its title/description — the numbers inside `snapshot` never recompute. This was live-verified directly: two Financial Summary reports generated minutes apart, around a new expense, show two different frozen totals side by side, exactly as "opening last month's report should still show last month's truth" requires.
- **Deleting is a real delete**, the one deliberate departure from this platform's usual archive-and-keep convention. Every other Record type (Contact, Project, Document, Position) archives rather than disappears, because it's the institution's own permanent memory. A Report is different — a disposable summary of facts that live elsewhere — and the brief named "Deleting reports" directly rather than "Archiving reports." Live-verified: a report deleted, gone from the list, its History narrated at the institution level (a deleted Report can't own its own Timeline once gone).

One new Area of Responsibility, `reports.manage`, gates generate/save/delete — the seventh independent confirmation that Governance & Responsibility Model v1's growth model (§11) needs zero new mechanism per application. Reading a Report and its Analytics stays open to everyone, matching the precedent every prior Be Aware aggregate already set (nobody has ever needed a permission just to see "12 active projects" on Home).

## Analytics — observations, not opinions

`applications/reports/analytics.ts` has no Core Model of its own, deliberately: it produces nothing that needs to persist, so it computes fresh every time it's asked, the same "reports own nothing" discipline applied to a second engine. Every `Observation` is a plain, countable fact, never a judgment:

- Unfilled positions
- Approvals waiting more than a week
- Documents expiring or expired within 30 days
- Relationships quiet for 90+ days (a genuinely different, earlier threshold than Attention's own 180-day nudge — Analytics' own early-warning value, not a duplicate)
- Projects blocked or past their target date
- **A real trend**: expenses risen or fallen, comparing the two most recently saved Financial Summary reports' expense totals

That last one is the one genuinely new mechanism this milestone needed: since Reports are already historical snapshots, comparing the two most recent ones of a matching category is an honest way to see a real trend without inventing a separate time-series store. It only fires with at least two saved Reports to compare — with fewer, it says nothing rather than guess. This was live-verified in both directions: generating a second Financial Summary report after a new ₹20,000 expense correctly produced "Expenses have risen from ₹5,000 to ₹25,000 since your last Financial Summary report," and after deleting one of the two reports, the same observation correctly disappeared — the comparison logic isn't just present, it's honestly gated on real data existing.

## Home — exactly one new signal, never spam

The brief's own instruction was explicit: "Only when meaningful. Never spam." Of the six Observation types Analytics can produce, exactly one became a new Act Now card: approvals stuck for over a week, surfaced as one aggregate item (not one card per stuck approval) and only to holders of `work.manage` — a genuinely new fact Work's own per-approval Attention items don't already say (those only show approvals *this* person can currently decide, never "these have been stuck too long" as a standalone fact). One new Be Aware line summarizes the current observation count with the single most notable one as its subtitle. Every other Observation stays visible only on the Reports page itself, in the Analytics section, exactly honoring "Reports answer what happened, Analytics answers what deserves attention — keep them separate": Analytics' full detail lives with Reports, Home gets only the one signal worth a founder's attention without a click.

## Charts — minimal, on purpose

Every `ReportChart` — regardless of its declared `kind` (bar/line/pie/trend, all named in the type) — renders through one component, `MiniBarChart`: a plain inline-SVG horizontal bar, no chart library. "Keep charts minimal... numbers first, charts second... no BI platform" was read as license to build one honest, legible shape instead of four thin ones. The `kind` field is real and ready for a genuine per-kind renderer later; nothing about how a snapshot is computed or stored would need to change to add one. Named directly in the Technical Debt Register as a real simplification, not hidden.

## Honest assessment — where this fell short of the full ask

- **Only one of the brief's seven trend examples was actually wired up as a real trend** (expenses rising/falling). The comparison mechanism itself is generic and real — it works by comparing metrics with matching labels between the two most recent saved Reports of a category — but extending it to "projects increasing in delays," "inactive members increasing," or "unfilled positions increasing" was not done this milestone, only proven possible. The other Analytics observations (unfilled positions, stuck approvals, expiring documents, quiet relationships, troubled projects) are all real, live, flat-fact counts today — genuinely useful, but not trends, despite reading similarly.
- **Multi-user walkthrough was not performed.** Only one real person (the founder) was available in the test institution. `reports.manage`'s gating was verified by code review and by the same pattern every prior Area already proved live in M6 through M10, not re-confirmed live here with a second person.
- **The "approvals waiting too long" Act Now card was not live-verified**, since simulating an approval genuinely seven days old requires either waiting a week or fabricating timestamps outside the normal action flow, neither of which this walkthrough did. The logic (`Date.now() - createdAt > 7 days`) was verified by code review and mirrors the identical, already-proven pattern Finance's warranty-expiry and Documents' expiry nudges both use.
- **Filters are honestly rough at the edges** — `status` and `type` are free-text inputs shared across all nine categories rather than a picker scoped to what's actually valid for the chosen one, so typing a Document status into a Work Summary silently filters to nothing rather than erroring. Named directly in the Technical Debt Register rather than smoothed over; building nine category-specific filter forms felt like exactly the BI-platform complexity the brief said not to build.

None of the above blocks anything. ARUMBU now has one real place a founder can ask "what happened" and get a frozen, trustworthy answer, and one real place — feeding Home sparingly, never as noise — that says what's worth a second look. Per the founder's own roadmap note: this is the last major institutional record built before Search (M12) can connect what now exists, before Tamizhi (M13) can reason across a complete platform instead of a half-built one.
